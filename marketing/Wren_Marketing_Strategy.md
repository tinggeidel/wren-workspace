# Wren — Marketing Strategy & Growth Playbook

*Owner: Ting · Created July 2026 · Goal: a warm, filled waitlist before public launch (3–6 months out)*
*Format: faceless account (no on-camera founder) · Handle: @meet.wren (IG) + matching TikTok*

---

## 0. The one-paragraph version

You are building an audience of women 22–45 who feel generic fitness/nutrition apps don't fit them, and converting the warmest of them into waitlist emails you can activate on launch day. You'll do it **faceless** — screen recordings, "asked my Coach" clips, myth-busting text-over-b-roll, and build-in-public — posting **short-form video daily-ish** to TikTok and Instagram Reels, all funneling to your live waitlist page. The whole thing runs on Wren's honest, education-first voice, because that voice is both what this audience trusts *and* what keeps Apple's App Review from rejecting you later. Volume and consistency beat polish. The target by launch: **~2–5k engaged followers and a waitlist in the hundreds-to-low-thousands.**

---

## 1. Positioning (what every post has to reinforce)

**Category:** cycle-aware training & nutrition, personalized from *your* data — not a body-scanner, not a medical app, not a generic fitness app.

**The wedge line (marketing):** *"Train with your cycle, not against it."*

**The honest promise (what makes it defensible):** Wren treats your cycle phase as a *starting guess* that your own logged energy and symptoms correct over time. It learns *you*. That's the personalization — and it lets you say "personalized from real data" and mean it literally.

**Who it's for:** women 22–45 who are tired of apps that treat them like a smaller man — MyFitnessPal's calorie guilt, generic programs that ignore that some weeks you feel unstoppable and some weeks you don't.

**Voice:** a smart, warm friend who happens to know the science and refuses to oversell it. Always *"many women find…"*, never *"you should."* Curious, not prescriptive. Honest about what the app can't see (it says food numbers are estimates, on purpose).

**Enemy (what we position against, never a competitor by name in a mean way):** one-size-fits-all fitness culture; the shame-and-restrict model; apps that pretend a photo can count grams of oil.

## 2. Non-negotiable content guardrails (read before every post)

These inherit directly from the app's ethos in the project plan. They are not stylistic preferences — they protect your users **and** your App Store approval (Apple reads your marketing/screenshots during review; confident medical/weight claims are a top rejection trigger for this category).

**Never:**
- Before/after body-transformation photos or "shrink your body" framing.
- Calorie-restriction hooks, weight-loss numbers ("lose 10 lbs"), or "hit your deficit" bait.
- Diagnosis-flavored claims — "fix your hormones," "balance your cortisol," "heal your metabolism."
- "You should eat/do X" prescriptions. Medical advice. Supplement-by-name recommendations.
- Fear hooks about food or bodies.

**Always:**
- "Many women find…" / "some people notice…" framing for anything cycle-related.
- Frame food numbers as *approximate*.
- Education-first: teach one honest thing per post.
- Include the spirit of "talk to your provider" when touching anything health-adjacent.
- Body-neutral, capability-focused language (energy, strength, feeling good — not size).

**Quick self-test before posting:** *"Could a woman in eating-disorder recovery watch this and feel worse?"* If maybe — rewrite it.

## 3. Platform strategy

**Primary two, cross-posted:** TikTok and Instagram Reels. (TikTok is confirmed operating in the US in 2026 under the new Oracle/Silver Lake/MGX joint venture, so it's safe to build there.) Same vertical video works on both; you film/edit once and post to both.

- **TikTok** = discovery engine. Best odds of a cold video reaching non-followers fast. This is where you find people.
- **Instagram Reels** = discovery *plus* your "storefront." @meet.wren's grid, bio, and Stories are where an interested person decides you're legit and taps the waitlist link. IG also has the better link-in-bio and DM-to-link tooling.

**Supporting (low effort, high leverage):**
- **Pinterest** — evergreen. Turn your best static "myth vs. reality" and phase-guide graphics into pins linking to the waitlist. Pinterest traffic compounds for months with near-zero extra work; the women's-wellness audience over-indexes here.
- **A niche community presence** — a couple of relevant Reddit/Facebook groups (e.g., women's strength training, cycle-syncing curious, PCOS-adjacent *general* wellness) where you show up as a helpful person, not a spammer. Soft mention of the waitlist only where rules allow.

**Explicitly deferred (don't spread thin):** YouTube long-form, a newsletter beyond the waitlist welcome flow, X/Twitter. Revisit after the two primary channels have traction.

**Faceless format library (your building blocks):**
1. **Screen-recording of the Coach** — the single most powerful asset you have. Real product, real answer, anonymized. "I asked Wren what to eat on a low-energy day." Screen-record, add captions, done.
2. **Text-over-b-roll** — a hook on screen over calm stock/lifestyle b-roll (someone stretching, cooking, walking). Voiceover optional (AI or your own voice; voice-only is *not* "on camera").
3. **Myth vs. reality cards** — animated text, two-panel. Great for myth-busting pillar and for Pinterest.
4. **Build-in-public screen clips** — a peek at the app, a design decision, "here's a bug I fixed," a poll. Humanizes a faceless account without a face.
5. **Talking-head alternative** — kinetic captions + voiceover + a subtle animated Wren mark. Carries "opinion" content without you on camera.

## 5. Cadence & workflow (built for ~2 hrs/week, compressed for 3–6 month runway)

Because launch is 3–6 months out, we front-load: **higher volume now** to build the audience while there's runway, then convert.

**Posting target:**
- **TikTok + IG Reels: 1 video/day, 5–6 days/week** (cross-posted, so that's ~5–6 *pieces* made per week). If that's too much, floor is 4/week — but in a compressed window, more at-bats = faster growth. Volume beats perfection; the algorithm rewards consistency.
- **IG Stories: 2–4 frames/week** (polls, behind-the-scenes, re-share your Reels).
- **Pinterest: 3–5 pins/week**, repurposed from your card-format posts. Batch monthly.

**Batch workflow (the sustainable version):**
1. **One 90-min batch session/week:** script + film/screen-record 5–6 videos at once (calendar in the companion doc removes the "what do I post" decision).
2. **One 30-min edit+caption pass:** cut, add captions (CapCut auto-captions), export.
3. **Schedule** everything (TikTok Scheduler + Meta Business Suite for IG). Posting becomes hands-off.
4. **15 min/day engagement window:** reply to every comment and DM in the first 60–90 min after posting (early engagement drives reach), and comment thoughtfully on 5–10 posts from adjacent creators.

**The two habits that matter most:** (1) never miss the batch session, (2) reply fast to early comments. Everything else is optional polish.

## 6. The funnel: content → waitlist (detailed kit in the companion doc)

Your page is live, so the job is **maximizing the click and the conversion**, not building the page.

**The path:** video (value) → soft CTA → link in bio / pinned comment → landing page → email captured → welcome email that sets expectations and asks for one share.

**Where the CTA lives:**
- **IG:** "link in bio." Pin one Reel that explains what Wren is with a clear "waitlist in bio" end card.
- **TikTok:** link in bio + say it in captions/pinned comment. TikTok suppresses obvious outbound pushes, so lead with value and make the link the *quiet* next step.
- **Every profile bio:** one line of positioning + "🔗 Join the waitlist."

**CTA phrasing (soft, on-brand — rotate):**
- "We're building this. Early access list's in the bio."
- "Want in when it opens? Waitlist's in the bio."
- "Comment PHASE and I'll send you the waitlist link." (DM automation — high converter on IG.)

**The "comment a keyword → DM the link" play** is your best faceless conversion mechanic on IG: it spikes comments (algorithm loves it) *and* delivers the link privately (higher click-through than bio). Use a tool like ManyChat or IG's native quick-replies.

**Micro-goal to instrument:** know your numbers even roughly — views → profile visits → link clicks → signups. If you can add a UTM to your waitlist link per platform, do it. (Share the URL and I'll set the UTMs up.)

## 7. Growth tactics beyond posting

- **Hook-first, always.** The first 1 second and first line of text decide everything. Every script in the calendar leads with the hook. Test 3 hook styles: question ("Why do some weeks at the gym feel impossible?"), pattern-interrupt ("Your fitness app was built for a man's body."), and curiosity ("I asked an AI coach the question I was too embarrassed to Google.").
- **Ride sounds & formats, not trends blindly.** Use trending audio *only* when it fits; a trending format (e.g., "green screen myth-bust") is more reusable than a trending dance.
- **Reply-to-comment videos.** When a comment asks a real question, answer it as its own video. Free, high-relevance content and it flatters the commenter.
- **Seed the "asked my Coach" series as a numbered series** ("Coach answers #7") — series train the algorithm and viewers to expect more, boosting follows.
- **Collaborate laterally.** Find 5–10 *non-competing* faceless or micro creators in women's wellness, cycle education, or strength training. Genuine comments now → collab/duet later. Avoid direct-competitor apps.
- **Pinterest as the compounding bet.** Pins keep driving waitlist traffic months after posting; it's the highest ROI-per-minute channel for evergreen wellness graphics.
- **Waitlist referral loop (post-signup):** the welcome email asks each signup to share with one friend who'd get it. Even a 10–15% share rate meaningfully lowers your effective cost per signup. (In the funnel doc.)
- **Build-in-public milestones as content:** "we just hit 500 on the waitlist," "beta tester said this," (anonymized, permissioned) — proof begets proof.

## 8. 90-day plan (mapped to your 3–6 month launch window)

**Weeks 1–2 — Foundation & first reps.**
Finalize bios on both platforms (see funnel doc). Pin an "explainer" Reel. Post 4–5×/week from the calendar. Goal: publish consistently, find your two or three best-performing formats. Don't judge results yet.

**Weeks 3–6 — Cadence & first signal.**
Push to 5–6×/week. Double down on whatever format got the most saves/shares (usually "asked my Coach"). Start the keyword→DM waitlist play. Begin Pinterest. Goal: first breakout video (>10k views), first steady waitlist trickle, ~500–1,500 followers.

**Weeks 7–10 — Funnel & proof.**
Now that you have reach, tighten conversion: every well-performing video gets an explicit-but-soft waitlist CTA. Add build-in-public "beta is happening" content to create urgency and FOMO. Collect anonymized beta reactions as proof content. Goal: waitlist growth accelerates, testimonials bank starts, ~1,500–3,000 followers.

**Weeks 11–13+ — Convert & prep launch.**
Introduce scarcity honestly ("first 500 off the waitlist get in first"). Warm the list with 2–3 "here's what's coming" emails. Line up a Product Hunt launch and 2–3 community posts for launch day. Goal: warm list ready to activate, ~2,000–5,000 followers, launch-day plan locked.

## 9. Metrics (what to actually track)

**Leading (weekly, tells you if it's working):**
- Posts published (did you hit cadence?) — the input you control.
- Views, and % from non-followers (reach health).
- Saves + shares per post (the real signal for this niche — saves > likes).
- Profile visits and link clicks.

**Lagging (the goal):**
- **Waitlist signups/week** — the north star.
- Follower growth (secondary — a means, not the end).
- Email open rate on the welcome flow (are these real, warm leads?).

**Targets by launch day (from the project plan):** ~2–5k engaged followers **and** a warm waitlist. A 3–5% "reach → waitlist" conversion on your best content is healthy; don't chase vanity follower counts over list quality.

**Kill/scale rule:** review every 2 weeks. Scale the format with the highest saves+shares. Quietly retire formats that get views but no saves and no clicks.

---

## 10. Risks & honest cautions

- **Burnout is the #1 risk** (per your own plan). The batch workflow and the ready-made calendar exist to protect against it. If you can only do one thing in a busy week, do the batch session; skip everything else.
- **Compliance drift.** As you get comfortable, it's tempting to reach for a spicier weight-loss hook because it'll pop. Don't — it risks your users and your App Review. The guardrails in §2 are the guardrails, always.
- **Don't over-optimize too early.** The first 2–3 weeks are for reps and learning, not conversion. Judge the funnel only after you have reach to funnel.
- **One platform could stall; that's fine.** Cross-posting means a TikTok dud might be an IG hit. Keep both running before concluding anything.
